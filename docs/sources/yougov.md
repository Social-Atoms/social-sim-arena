# Economist / YouGov

**What the number is.** A weekly survey wave of YouGov's own online panel of US adults, published jointly with The Economist. The arena scores three things from it: the share of US adult citizens who approve of the President's job performance (the topline, in percent, adults not registered voters), the generic congressional ballot margin (Democratic percent minus Republican percent, in points, positive means Democrats lead), and the approval crosstabs, sixteen subgroup cells scored together as one profile. Approve and disapprove do not sum to 100; a "not sure" residual of three to four points remains.

**Where it is published.** Fielded over a weekend and published midweek, roughly 1,400 to 1,700 respondents per wave, as a topline PDF and a public tracker on [yougov.com](https://yougov.com/en-us/topics/topic/The_Economist_YouGov_polls). The same pollster also files polls sponsored by CBS News and Yahoo News; those are other surveys on other schedules and are excluded.

**How the arena reads it.** The topline and the generic ballot come from the Silver Bulletin poll database (see [silver-bulletin.md](silver-bulletin.md)) filtered to pollster YouGov, sponsor Economist, and, for approval, the adult population; the raw marginals, never anyone's adjusted figure. The series are declared in [`ssa/series.py`](https://github.com/Social-Atoms/social-sim-arena/blob/main/ssa/series.py). The crosstabs come from YouGov's public tracker workbook, one sheet per subgroup, six rows per sheet (wave dates, approve, disapprove, not sure, unweighted and weighted base), values rounded to whole points. Every pull is archived as a dated vintage under `sources/yougov_xtab/` and the reader, [`ssa/adapters/yougov_xtab.py`](https://github.com/Social-Atoms/social-sim-arena/blob/main/ssa/adapters/yougov_xtab.py), reads the newest vintage, never the network.

**How a question resolves.** Approval and generic ballot resolve on the topline of the first wave published after the lock. A crosstab question names its wave: each cell resolves on that cell's approve percentage in the single wave the workbook dates inside the seven days the question covers, and it waits, by name, until a vintage carrying that wave is committed.

**What to watch.** YouGov's public-data licence forbids automated extraction without written permission, and a request is with their legal team, so the workbook is pulled by hand (`python3 tools/pull_yougov_xtab.py --execute`), about once a week, never by the six-hourly refresh. The weekly movement of a cell is mostly measurement noise (about 0.8 points on the topline and up to 4.8 on the smallest cell), which is why the crosstabs are scored as a profile and each cell's noise floor is published beside its score; a cell whose base is an order of magnitude smaller than the rest (Other, median base 69) is excluded by name.
