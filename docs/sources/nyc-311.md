# NYC 311 service requests (NYC Open Data)

**What the number is.** How many 311 service requests New York City logged in one Monday-to-Sunday week, New York local time: every row of the dataset, all complaint types, responding agencies, statuses and channels, citywide. The rules are the approved Data Card [`nyc-311-requests@v2`](../data-cards/nyc-311-requests.json) (sha256 `ea4176dc4eb4c1c46084c38f85419a53f620325a52877942c90a60ffd5774e22`). The weekly count has risen about 6% a year: it averaged 69,803 in 2025 and 77,033 in 2026 so far. Over the 52 weeks to 2026-11-01 it ran from 57,403 (Christmas 2025) to 111,196 (the snow-and-heat week of 2026-01-26).

**Where it is published.** [311 Service Requests from 2020 to Present](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/d/erm2-nwe9) (`erm2-nwe9`) on NYC Open Data, published by NYC 311 and the Office of Technology and Innovation, updated daily (metadata: `Automation: Yes`), keyless through the Socrata SODA API. There is **no release calendar**: the data loads once a day at about 02:40Z, each load carrying rows to about 02:00 local of the day before, with occasional skipped days that the next load catches up. So SSA both sets this metric and resolves it (docs/sources.md §1.1), and the definition is the committed card. NYC Administrative Code §23-502 publishes city data sets for automated processing without restrictions on use, asking that the source and version be identified: *Contains NYC 311 / NYC Open Data, 311 Service Requests from 2020 to Present (erm2-nwe9), as archived by the arena on the date in each file name, unmodified.* The portal's Terms of Use answered 403 to automated reads, so a maintainer reads them in a browser before this source goes live (card, PERSON TASK).

**How the arena reads it.** [`ssa/adapters/nyc311.py`](../../ssa/adapters/nyc311.py) sends one grouped request a day, `count(*)` by created day and agency for the trailing 280 days, from the refresh workflow (non-blocking; `nyc311-capture.yml` adds two Monday retries at 02:17Z and 03:17Z, because only Monday's capture is inside the 24 hours before the lock), and files the body exactly as served under `sources/nyc311/<fetch day, UTC>.json`, write-once per day. Each capture's fetch time, URL, sha256, size and the `X-SODA2-Data-Out-Of-Date`, `X-SODA2-Truth-Last-Modified` and `Last-Modified` headers are kept in `sources/nyc311/fetches.json`. Socrata sometimes answers from a replica days behind its source and says so in `X-SODA2-Data-Out-Of-Date: true`; such a body is refused and never filed, and the next scheduled run tries again. The timeout is 180 s, because those replica answers take 60-70 s. If maintainers rule that automated retrieval is not allowed, a named person downloads the same URL every Sunday 08:00-20:00 ET and files it with [`tools/ingest_nyc311.py`](../../tools/ingest_nyc311.py), which proves freshness from the content (the newest day posted in full must be the Friday) since a browser save carries no headers.

**How a question resolves.** Forecasts lock Sunday 23:59 ET before the week. The ranked reference (`zero_point` `mean4`, one setting of the shared `mean_k` family) is fixed at lock from the fresh capture of the 24 hours before it: the mean of the four settled weeks starting D-35 to D-14, never the week that ends at the lock, scored as a normal whose sd is the root-mean-square error of the same rule over the 13 most recent settled weeks in that capture. If a reference week fails the outage check the block steps back one week, and if it still fails the round is not posted. Persistence on the week D-14 is shown, unranked. The week resolves on the first fresh capture at or after Tuesday 12:00 ET eight days after the week ends whose truth time is after Monday 18:00 ET. A target day under 40% of its 28-day median, or with no rows for NYPD, HPD, DSNY, DOT or DEP, moves the resolution to the next fresh capture until Friday 12:00 ET, and after that the round is cancelled, never resolved from a later pull. In manual mode the round resolves on the Sunday download six days after the week ends, with a late download accepted until Wednesday 12:00 ET. A round states its mode when posted and never switches.

**What to watch.** *Late fill-in was measured, not piloted*: 41 weeks of row insert times put the share still missing at resolution at a median of 0.003% (max 0.73%). One confirmation check stands in for a pilot: on 2026-12-11 a maintainer runs `python -m ssa.adapters.nyc311 fillin 2026-11-23`, and growth of 0.5% or more between the +8 and +11 day snapshots means the first scored round (lock 2026-12-13) runs unscored. *Weather and holidays are the hard weeks*: snow, heat and noise bursts move a week by tens of thousands, and every simple reference misses Christmas. *The data is a backfill*: the dataset was split and re-loaded on 2025-12-26, and history read today is not what was first posted. *Datacenter access is untested*: a 403 or 429 on the refresh runners means the capture moves to a local courier. *The year-over-year reference cannot be shown* from a 280-day capture; see the open items in the pull request.
