# BLS CPI: rent of primary residence (US Bureau of Labor Statistics)

**What the number is.** The one-month percent change in the seasonally adjusted CPI-U index for rent of primary residence, US city average (series `CUSR0000SEHA`), computed as 100 × (L_M / L_{M-1} − 1) from the two published index levels, unrounded; the BLS press table prints one decimal. It measures contract rents paid by tenants in the CPI housing sample, not asking rents, and each unit is re-priced every six months, so the series is smooth: the ranked reference misses by about 0.06 points a month. National only at launch; a ten-metro profile of the not-seasonally-adjusted metro indexes is future work. The rules are the approved Data Card [`bls-cpi-rent@v2`](../data-cards/bls-cpi-rent.json) (sha256 `9a5c42d2fc293ea433cd78590908b95dc017a7b76817ff6fbd2bdfa7b390d4e7`) of the Urban Track (housing).

**Where it is published.** The monthly CPI news release at 08:30 ET, on a schedule BLS announces for the calendar year ([bls.gov/schedule/news_release/cpi.htm](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/cpi.htm)), and the keyless [BLS Public Data API v1](https://api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v1/timeseries/data/). CPI data are a work of the US government (17 U.S.C. §105). Cite *Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics.*

**How the arena reads it.** [`ssa/adapters/bls_cpi.py`](../../ssa/adapters/bls_cpi.py) sends one POST for `CUSR0000SEHA`, years Y−3 to Y, and files the body exactly as served under `sources/bls_cpi/<YYYY-MM-DDTHHMMZ>.json`, with `<stamp>.meta.json` beside it (fetch instant, request JSON, sha256, size, newest month). At most one capture a UTC day, from the six-hourly refresh (non-blocking step); on a CPI release day, `.github/workflows/bls-cpi.yml` runs the same command at 08:45 ET (12:45 and 13:45 UTC) and keeps capturing until one holds the new month, never more than six a day. The series is read from the newest capture, never the network. A month BLS did not publish (`-`, October 2025) has no level, so it and the month after have no change and are skipped.

**How a question resolves.** On the first archived capture at or after the 08:30 ET release whose newest period is M, both levels from that same response; later revisions, including each February's seasonal re-estimation, never change it. A release cancelled or delayed past release + 45 days, or a missing M−1 level, cancels the round. The ranked reference is `zero_point` `mean6`: the mean of the six most recent one-month changes known at lock (missing months skipped, searching eight back), with sd the RMS error of the same rule over the 12 most recent past targets (searching 14 back); a capture from the 24 hours before the lock must carry M−1, or the round posts no reference. Rounds lock at release − 48 h, 08:30 America/New_York. Proposed rounds, two unscored pilots and the first scored round: [`bls-cpi-rent.rounds.json`](../data-cards/bls-cpi-rent.rounds.json).

**What to watch.** *Runner access is unmeasured*: www.bls.gov and download.bls.gov answered 403 to an honest client from the research sandbox while the API answered 200; a 403 from GitHub's runners moves the capture to a local courier. *The 2027 release dates are estimates* until a person copies the BLS 2027 schedule into `bls_cpi.RELEASES` and the rounds (bls.gov answered 403 to the research pass). *Shocks happen*: April 2026 printed +0.545% against a reference of 0.224, after BLS warned that the October 2025 gap in the six-month panel would make it atypical.
