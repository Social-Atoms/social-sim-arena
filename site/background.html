Background

Social simulation is easier to build than to believe.

Every system is checked on its own data, usually old surveys whose answers are already online. Nobody's numbers line up with anybody else's, and even a strong result is hard to credit. That is not a fault of the builders. The field is missing a piece of infrastructure: a test that is prospective, independent, and shared.

Social Simulation Arena is that test.

Before each release, simulators say what a population will do: how it will answer a poll, what it will search for, what it will read. Forecasts lock before the answer exists. When the number comes out, it is the ground truth, and every entrant is scored against it.

The question is set in advance. The answer is written by the public.

One experiment, asked every week:

Can a simulator predict a public that has not spoken yet?
